six layers

      
every layer is real.

      
most "private" launchpads tell a story. this one runs code you can check. each layer below says what it protects, and what stays public anyway.

      

        

        

          
01
a site that tracks nothing
no analytics, no cookies, no third-party scripts or fonts. the page only talks to its own server, and its security headers forbid anything else. open devtools and check.
your IP reaches our host, like every website.

          
02
no wallet connect
you never connect a wallet, approve anything, or sign a launch. there is nothing for a drainer to hijack and no wallet of yours on the launch transaction.
the coin, its name, image and description.

          
03
a creator key born in your browser
your browser makes a brand-new key, and that key becomes the coin's creator. it has no history and no funding trail. we never see it. you save it, you own the fees.
the creator address and the fees it earns.

          
04
a fresh launcher every time
a new wallet signs each launch. the halo treasury funds it inside the same transaction to the lamport, so it ends empty. the dev wallet on the chart is never yours.
that the halo treasury paid for the launch.

          
05
claims without gas
funding a new wallet is how people get traced. so the creator key never needs funding: you sign the claim in your browser and the treasury pays the network fee.
the claim, and anything you do with the fees after.

          
06
clean files
your image is redrawn in your browser before upload, which drops hidden data like camera, time and location. only the pixels leave your device.
whatever the picture itself shows.

        

      

      
what no launchpad can hide: solana is a public ledger. the coin, every trade, the creator address and every movement of its fees are visible to anyone. halo makes sure none of it points back to you — as long as you never link the creator key to your own wallet.

readme.txt

      
the fine print.

      

        
what is halo?
a pump.fun launchpad built around privacy you can check. you fill in a coin, your browser makes the creator key, halo's treasury pays the creation cost and a fresh wallet signs the launch. you never connect, sign or pay.

        
is it really free?
yes. creating a pump.fun coin costs about 0.02 SOL in account rent and network fees. the halo treasury pays it. there is no dev buy, so the coin starts with nobody holding it.

        
why is there a daily limit?
every launch costs the treasury real SOL, so there are 20 free launches per day (resets 00:00 UTC) and a few per visitor. instead of an account you do a few seconds of proof of work in your browser. it keeps bots from emptying the treasury without asking who you are.

        
who gets the creator fees?
the creator key from step 3, and only it. pump.fun pays creator fees to that address on every trade, and halo takes no cut. claim them in claim: you sign with the key, the treasury pays the gas.

        
can halo take my fees or my coin?
no. halo never sees the creator key, it never buys the coin, and the launch wallet ends with zero SOL. what halo does hold is its own treasury key, which only pays launch costs and claim fees.

        
what if i lose the creator key?
the fees stay where they are, unclaimable. nobody can recover the key, including us. that is the price of a key nobody else ever had.

        
what can people still see?
everything on solana is public: the coin, trades, the creator address, the launch wallet and that the halo treasury funded it. what they cannot see is a link to you, unless you make one, for example by sending the fees to a wallet you use in public.

        
does halo keep logs?
halo stores each launch (the coin details and addresses, which are public anyway) and, for the daily limit only, a salted hash of your IP that becomes meaningless the next day and is then deleted. no accounts, no cookies, no analytics. the host still sees requests the way every host does.

        
is this financial advice?
no. memecoins are extremely risky and most go to zero. halo is a tool. it is not affiliated with pump.fun.

        
is there a $HALO token?
not yet. when there is, its contract address appears at the bottom of this page and on our X.
